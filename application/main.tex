\section{Project Summary}
%\lengthnote{max 1/2 page (per the call)}

%\textcolor{blue}{Critical infrastructure is increasingly affected by aging, climate exposure, water ingress, and limited maintenance resources, while bridge inspection generates growing volumes of heterogeneous information from Unmanned Aerial Systems (UAS), sensors, inspection reports, and 3D models. The emerging challenge is to support engineers what the combined evidence means, how certain that interpretation is, and what steps should be taken next, in collaborative workflows that combine Artificial Intelligence (AI) with engineering expertise. 
This PhD project will investigate human--AI interaction for bridge Structural Health Monitoring (SHM). The framework will integrate Unmanned Aerial Systems (UAS) imagery, sensor measurements, structural information, inspection histories, environmental observations, and engineering knowledge through a Digital Twin (DT). Artificial Intelligence (AI) based on Large Language Models (LLM) and Vision-Language Models (VLM) will support interpretation of this evidence by identifying relevant relationships, plausible deterioration mechanisms, uncertainty, and information gaps. The scientific contribution is a human--AI decision paradigm. Evidence is semantically contextualized into an evolving representation of the bridge condition described as a Knowledge Graph (KG).%, interpreted by AI-to support reasoning, and validated by engineers before it informs inspection or maintenance decisions. 
The framework will be validated experimentally by comparing conventional engineering workflows, AI assistance without semantic grounding, and the proposed knowledge-grounded human--AI workflow. The framework will be validated on two bridge case studies in the Global South, namely, the Ponte do Funil (Itaparica Island, Bahia, Brazil), supported by the Federal University of Bahia, and the elevated viaduct sections of the Nairobi Expressway (Nairobi, Kenya), supported by the University of Nairobi. Deterioration mechanisms associated with water ingress, expansion joints, drainage deficiencies, moisture, cracking, and corrosion-related symptoms will be prioritized. %Transferability to contexts with different climatic conditions, data availability, monitoring capabilities, and resource constraints.% This comparison will also assess the approach's applicability in developing countries, where infrastructure owners may face limited monitoring technologies, incomplete historical data, and constrained inspection and maintenance resources.}

\subsection{Motivation, Significance and Scientific Challenges}
%\subsubsection{Motivation}
Deterioration of bridge infrastructure is a critical issue in Western countries, where such assets, in large part, already exceed their nominal life. %Europe has a very dense network of transportation infrastructures. The prioritization of what to repair and what to demolish is a critical challenge. 
The catastrophic collapse of the Morandi Bridge (Genova, Italy, 2018) indicates that bridges in poor condition may still be operated. The Global South is characterized by younger infrastructure; however, underfunded maintenance programs are responsible for faster deterioration. Vibration-based SHM has been implemented extensively in the last 20 years along with the large diffusion of cost-effective sensing technology~\cite{figueiredo_three_2022}. More recently, UASs have become affordable and easy to operate, facilitating the capture of high-resolution image data from hard-to-access structural elements~\cite{panigati_drone-based_2025}. However, engineers must still combine these heterogeneous sources to determine whether an observed condition is significant, whether additional inspection is needed, and which intervention to prioritize~\cite{hoskere_unified_2025}. The overwhelming amount of data, along with the shortage of qualified personnel, renders this a challenging task. \par

\subsection{State of the Art}
%\subsubsection{Digital twins}
DTs are the natural evolutions of SHM systems~\cite{talasila_digital_2026}. A DT combines digital and physical assets into a cyber-physical system (CPS) to deliver specific services~\cite{fitzgerald_engineering_2024}. DTs for SHM today are capable of collecting multi-modal data (e.g., vibration and image data) in an almost automated manner~\cite{hoskere_unified_2025}. Machine Learning (ML) provides a workflow to automate specific processing tasks (e.g., crack localization). However, decision making regarding inspection and maintenance requires expert users to reason across large data sets considering the entire history of the asset~\cite{celik_vision_2026}. It is recognized in the community that such an approach leaves substantial information unexploited. Recent work points toward the use of AI to overcome this limit~\cite{luo_chattwin_2026}.\par
Ontologies are being explored to add a semantic description to DT components and functionalities and facilitate user interaction. This is fundamental for Situational Awareness (SA): engineers must not only access heterogeneous information, but also perceive changes in structural condition, understand their implications, and anticipate their evolution~\cite{lappalainen_user_2024}. An ontology provides a formal, machine-interpretable representation of domain concepts and their relationships~\cite{ghidalia_combining_2024}. DTs therefore increasingly employ ontologies as semantic layers for interoperability, traceability, and machine reasoning~\cite{li_comprehensive_2024,jakel_system_2025}. Therefore, they have been recently investigated in for for turning fragmented inspection and maintenance data into structured knowledge for condition assessment of bridges~\cite{jiang_machine_2023,li_human---loop_2026}. In parallel, conversational agents and ontologies are explored to operate a DT in place of a graphical user interface~\cite{li_human---loop_2026}. \par
XR, which comprises immersive technologies such as Virtual Reality (VR), Augmented Reality (AR), and Mixed Reality (MR), shows great promise for enhancing DTs, both as a gateway to experiencing data that simulate or extend the real world~\cite{pandey2025digital} and as a means of improving usability~\cite{vona2025user}, collaboration and decision-making \cite{sthapit2026digital}. XR is also being explored as a way to enhance the manual bridge inspection process independently of DTs~\cite{smith_wearable_2022, gabbard_inspectar_2025}. Immersive DT environments have been combined with interactive virtual-agent interfaces for infrastructure leak simulation and monitoring \cite{marzban_immersive_2025}.\par
Nevertheless, important gaps remain. The outlined state of the art is fragmented, single-technology-centered, and engineers SA is overlooked \cite{celik_vision_2026}. Human involvement is limited to validate AI outputs, refine generated knowledge, or facilitate interaction with digital information \cite{li_human---loop_2026,luo_chattwin_2026}, rather than being explicitly modeled as part of a joint human--AI reasoning and decision-making system.

A central research challenge will be preventing the AI from presenting unsupported conclusions as established facts. A semantic description of the evolving bridge condition will constrain and contextualize AI reasoning, providing engineering-sound assessments of the bridge state and evidence for decision support. For example, when moisture is detected below an expansion joint, the AI retrieves the joint condition and inspection history, drainage configuration, recent rainfall exposure, nearby cracking or corrosion observations, and relevant engineering knowledge before proposing plausible deterioration hypotheses. 
%%The novelty of this architecture is that it explicitly distinguishes observed evidence, retrieved knowledge, inferred relationships, and hypotheses. %It will communicate uncertainty, provide evidence provenance, identify competing explanations, and explicitly indicate missing information. In this project, the DT is an evolving representation that integrates BIM/FEM models with inspection observations, sensor measurements, environmental information, historical records, deterioration states, and maintenance information. The semantic layer that connects these heterogeneous representations is implemented as a Knowledge Graph (KG). %As an example, the observation of a damage feature (e.g., a localized crack) will be recorded as contextualized empirical evidence describing \textit{what} was observed, \textit{when} it occurred, \textit{which} structural component it concerns. AI will be able to guess \textit{which sources} are likely to have produced the evidence, and its relationship with previous observations.
This will allow the DT to evolve as new evidence becomes available and provide a machine-interpretable representation of the infrastructure situation. \pagemark{Sustainability -- start}
\section{Sustainability goals of the project and relation to the CEBE program vision}
%\lengthnote{max 1/2 page (per the call)}
%\lengthnote{Specific sustainability objectives taking into account possible sustainability-related trade-offs, Sustainability-related conceptual and methodological choices}

\justifying
\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0.8em}

\projectname contributes to CEBE vision of a built environment in harmony with planetary boundaries by addressing risk-informed life extension (Gap 7, RF7) based on timely and trustworthy interpretation of deterioration. In bridges, much avoidable material use and CO$_2$ stems from late detection. Failing expansion joints and drainage let water and chlorides reach the reinforcement, and a local repair escalates into deck rehabilitation or replacement.\par 
%
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\textbf{Objectives.} (O1) \textit{Least intervention:} \projectname supports decisions (inspect, repair, replace) that select the minimum action consistent with safety. (O2) \textit{Information sufficiency:} \projectname identifies the minimum information needed for a trustworthy assessment, measurements and computation are added only where they change the decision.\par 
%
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\textbf{Trade-offs and methodological choices.} Digitalization is not sustainable per se. LLM/VLM inference consumes energy and resources. \projectname therefore adopts a fit-for-purpose principle. Existing records are exploited before new data are acquired. Postponing action is sustainable only if deterioration is not underestimated. The separation of evidence, prior knowledge and inference, and engineer oversight are the safeguards against automation bias.\par 
%
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\textbf{SDG perspective:} Bridge infrastructures connect settlements and guaranty the delivery of goods and services. The rise in temperature and increase in rainfall negatively impact the corrosion mechanisms affecting common construction materials like concrete and steel. Therefore \projectname SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities) and SDG 13 (Climate Action).
